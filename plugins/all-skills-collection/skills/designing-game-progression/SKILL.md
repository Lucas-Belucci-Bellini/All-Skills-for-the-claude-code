# Designing Game Progression

## Goal
Create a progression structure where each new capability, challenge, or reward reinforces the game's core loop without overwhelming the player or making earlier choices irrelevant.

## Procedure
1. State the intended player fantasy and the core actions progression should deepen.
2. Map the experience into milestones. For each milestone, write what the player knows, can do, is challenged by, and is expected to understand next.
3. Separate progression dimensions: skill mastery, narrative, access/unlocks, character power, collection, and social status. Identify dependencies and avoid making one dimension carry every reward.
4. Introduce one major mechanic at a time where possible. Pair new mechanics with safe practice before combining them under pressure.
5. Define the purpose of every reward: new decision, expression, convenience, mastery signal, story reveal, or resource. Remove rewards with no clear player-facing purpose.
6. Check pacing with a milestone table: expected duration, prerequisite, new demand, reward, failure recovery, and optional route.
7. Test edge cases: players who skip optional content, lose repeatedly, optimize a single strategy, return after a long break, or reach content out of order.
8. Review power growth. If numbers increase, verify that enemy demands, resource costs, and encounter readability still create decisions rather than inflated health bars.
9. Play through a representative slice and record where the player is confused, bored, overloaded, or no longer challenged.
10. Revise one pacing or dependency variable at a time and document the trade-off.

## Deliverable
- Progression map with milestones and prerequisites
- New mechanic and player-learning objective per milestone
- Reward purpose and expected duration
- Catch-up, failure recovery, and optional-content rules
- Risks, assumptions, and test observations

## Acceptance criteria
- Each milestone has a clear player-facing purpose.
- New mechanics are introduced before being combined at high pressure.
- Optional paths do not silently block required progress.
- Progression remains understandable after a break or skipped content.
- Balance assumptions are marked as untested until measured.

## Avoid
- Rewarding time spent when the design intends to reward mastery.
- Using grind to hide a lack of meaningful decisions.
- Making permanent power increases invalidate previous content.
- Treating a spreadsheet as proof that pacing feels good in play.
